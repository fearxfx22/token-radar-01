How the value is worked out

Every ranked protocol is scored by the same function, from the snapshot’s own figures only: TVL, age, 7-day growth, chain count, category. No protocol name is special-cased. The implied value is an expected value: the value if a token launched, times the odds that one ever launches.

implied = TVL × fitted multiple × P(launch eventually)

The diagram below walks through the top protocol, so you can check every step against the snapshot.

  • fitted multiple: a ridge regression of ln(market cap) on ln TVL, ln age, 7-day TVL change (clipped to −30% / +100%), ln chain count, and the ten most common categories, fitted on the 128 live protocols with a positive market cap (penalty λ = 30). The market pays less per TVL dollar for the biggest protocols: the fitted ln TVL slope is 0.47, so a protocol ten times the median TVL gets roughly a third of the multiple, and one ten times smaller gets roughly 3.4 times the multiple. The growth term is clipped because the snapshot has rows at −72.7% and +4448% in a week, and an unclipped collapse would inflate the multiple — the unclipped fit put a protocol that had just lost 73% of its TVL at rank 1 with a 4.6x multiple.
  • P(launch eventually): the forward odds that a protocol which has run this long with no token ever launches one. The share of protocols that have launched by age a is fitted as F(a) = 0.425 × (1 − exp(−a / 950 days)) by maximum likelihood over all 758 protocols; the forward odds are (0.425 − F(a)) ÷ (1 − F(a)), floored at 0.02 and capped at 0.90. The odds fall with age — 0.40 at 90 days, 0.34 at a year, 0.26 at two years, 0.10 at five years — because a protocol that has run for years without a token is less likely to ever launch one than a young one. A cross-sectional rate would say the opposite, because older protocols have had more time to launch; that is why the category-median odds of radar-5 were replaced.

Validation: does the fitted model beat the TVL-only line out of sample?

Fixed splits, decided before modelling: a locked holdout of 26 live protocols, 5-fold and 10-fold cross-validation on the other 102, and a walk-forward split (train on protocols listed before 2024, test on those listed after). The snapshot’s figures explain only about 20% of log market-cap variance — that is the data’s ceiling, not the model’s. 10-fold CV: ridge 0.14 vs the TVL-only line 0.15, a wash. 5-fold: ridge 0.20 vs the line 0.17. The locked holdout favours the simple line outright (R² 0.22, MSE 4.41, Spearman 0.53) against the ridge (R² 0.15, MSE 4.83, Spearman 0.45). Walk-forward is the one split the ridge wins: +0.05 against −0.06 for the line. The simple fit goes negative out of time; the ridge stays positive — and a board is a forward bet, so the time-robust model ships, not because its random-split fit is better but because it survives the direction the board actually faces.

The honest limit: the launch odds cannot be validated out of sample on this snapshot, because no launch dates are recorded, so there is no held-out launch to predict. The saturating curve they derive from is validated — 5-fold CV log-loss 0.49, tied with a logistic on age and TVL (0.48) and better than the base rate (0.51) — but the forward odds themselves are a peer-history estimate, not a validated prediction, and no score for them is invented here.

What changed from radar-5, and why: the TVL-only multiple is gone — the ridge now fits every value term the snapshot carries (TVL, age, 7-day growth, chain count, category), with the growth term clipped to [−30%, +100%] so one wild week cannot dominate a multiple. The category-median launch odds are gone — replaced by forward odds from a saturating launch curve, because a cross-sectional rate rewards old protocols for having had more time to launch, which is the wrong direction for a board. The board grew from 206 to 340 ranked rows, and the 274 pre-token protocols that fail the existence screens are now listed in Set aside with their reasons instead of being silently dropped.

What is ranked, and what is not

  • Ranked (340): all 40 watchlist protocols, plus every pre-token protocol below the $50M floor that passes the board’s existence screens: no CEX, Bridge, Canonical Bridge, Liquid Staking, Liquid Restaking or Restaked BTC category; no versioned name; no whole word wrapped, staked or staking. A versioned name is a version of a protocol whose token already trades (Aave V3, Uniswap V4), and a CEX or a bridge is not a launch of its own.
  • Not ranked: protocols that already trade (shown in Live tokens at their market caps), and the 274 pre-token protocols that fail an existence screen (shown in Set aside with the reason).
  • The honest caveat: “records no token” is what the snapshot says, not a promise that no token exists. An implied value is this radar’s claim about what a launch could be worth, priced on the live market, but a claim all the same. Farm plans assume no token is confirmed.

Farm plans

Every ranked protocol carries an eight-step plan, derived from its own record (category, first chain, TVL, age), never from a hand-written list of names. Capital is a flat $150 of USDC; gas is priced per chain (Ethereum ~$8, other chains ~$0.50, a bridge ~$8.50, ~$1 a week); the hold is 30 days. Each step states its action, its cost and its time, and the step-8 total is computed from the same numbers, so it always reconciles with steps 2–4. Points programs are named only where verified live on 2026-11-23 (11 programs); 28 protocols were individually checked with no public program found; every other row says to check the protocol’s own points page instead of claiming a program status that was not audited.
