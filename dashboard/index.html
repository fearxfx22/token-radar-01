How the numbers are made — and how they hold up

Value fit: trimmed OLS vs the alternatives

The shipped model is T-OLS: OLS of log mcap on log TVL, fitted on the 94 comps whose mcap/TVL lies in [0.02, 2]. The other 34 comps are extreme multiples (0.00025× to 400×) that no TVL-only model can price; the fit deliberately does not try. Every row below is computed at build time from the snapshot on the locked splits (holdout 26, selection 102, 5 folds seed 7). miss = exp(mean |log error|) — a ×2.8 miss means the typical prediction is within 2.8× either way.

Launch odds: does a protocol this old, this big, growing this fast have a token?

Ridge logistic (λ=0.1) on [ln TVL, log age, clipped growth], fitted on the 645 dated protocols. Target: a token ticker exists (symbol non-null) — 327 of 645 dated protocols, base rate 50.7%. Brier is the mean squared error of the predicted probability; lower beats the constant base-rate guess.

Uncertainty bands: what the fitted value could actually be

Bands come from leave-one-out residuals of the shipped fit on the 94 trimmed comps. The P25–P75 band [0.423×, 2.806×] is the middle half of what the model gets wrong; the P10–P90 band [0.222×, 4.991×] is the middle 80%. Coverage is measured on held-out rows, not on the fit itself.

Ablation: what happens when you change the model

LOO-full and walk-forward miss on all 128 comps (the full set, extremes included). The losers are kept on purpose: growth and age do not help the value fit out of sample — permuting growth or shuffling age barely moves the miss, and adding them (T-OLS-FA) makes walk-forward worse. Category dummies do not help either. Standardizing the ridge helps walk-forward but is not shipped: it changes the coefficients' meaning and the gain is inside the noise. The shipped T-OLS is the simplest model that wins the locked holdout.

Cap on TVL: the fitted multiple falls as protocols get bigger

Each dot is a live comparable (token-bearing protocol): x = TVL, y = mcap/TVL. The line is the shipped fit. Big protocols are worth a smaller multiple of TVL than small ones — that is why the farm pays best at the small end, and why the board's giants (rank 1–6) are SKIP at a $150 deposit even though they are the most valuable protocols.

Farm economics: the $150 deposit, costed to the dollar

Drop pool = 7% of implied value. Farmer share = $150 ÷ (participation × TVL), participation 5% by default. Costs: bridge $8.50 + gas (ETH $8.00, SOL $0.05, BNB $0.10, other $0.50) + $1/week × 5 weeks. Net = pool × share − costs. Verdict: FARM if the band-low net clears $150; MARGINAL if the point net is positive; SKIP otherwise. At a $150 deposit no row clears $150 at band-low — FARM is empty, honestly. MIN DEP is the deposit that would clear it. Sensitivity shows the net if only 2% (or 10%) of TVL is farmed.
